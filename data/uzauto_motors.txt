== Overview ==
UzAuto Motors, formerly GM Uzbekistan, is an automobile manufacturer owned by the Government of Uzbekistan and based in Asaka. It produces vehicles under the Chevrolet brand and previously also under the Ravon brand. The company was once partly owned by General Motors. In 2019 the government acquired it and renamed it UzAuto Motors. By February 2023 the company had completed an IPO on the Tashkent Stock Exchange.

== Daewoo era ==
The company was founded in 1992 by the state-owned UzAvtosanoat and the South Korean company Daewoo, originally under the name Uz-DaewooAuto. The idea came in the early 1990s under President Islam Karimov. Production began on 19 July 1996 at the new plant in Asaka. Uz-Daewoo branded vehicles were produced until 2015.

== GM Uzbekistan (2008-2019) ==
After Daewoo collapsed in 2001 and was acquired by General Motors, GM Uzbekistan was created in March 2008 as a joint venture in which UzAvtosanoat held 75% and General Motors held 25%. Production started on 27 November 2008, and the first car produced was a Chevrolet Lacetti. The Nexia and Matiz were sold under the Chevrolet badge. In the first half of 2011 the company produced 106,334 vehicles.
An engine plant in Tashkent opened in November 2011 with a capacity of 225,000 engines per year. In 2014 a plant in Pitnak (Khorezm Region) opened to produce Damas and Labo microvans and the Chevrolet Orlando.
In 2010 about 5,000 people worked at the plant. In 2011 the company sold 121,584 vehicles in the domestic market and also exported cars to Russia and other CIS countries.

== Government ownership and recent history ==
In 2019 the Government of Uzbekistan acquired the company and renamed it UzAuto Motors.
In 2020 it started importing several Chevrolet SUVs and phased out the Ravon brand, moving its models under the Chevrolet brand.
In April 2021 it announced plans to produce Chevrolet vehicles for Russia.
In August 2021 the Swedish businessman Bo Andersson was appointed CEO.
From 2022 exports to Russia and Belarus stopped because of sanctions, and exports to Ukraine were temporarily halted.
In October 2022 the company announced a domestic IPO, one of three planned "People's IPOs".
In 2022 Fitch upgraded the company's rating to BB- with a stable outlook.
The article also notes that the company's dominant market position leaves buyers with few choices, and that high import tariffs limit imports of foreign car brands.
