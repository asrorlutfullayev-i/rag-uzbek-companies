== Overview ==
JSC Uzbekistan Airways is the national flag carrier of Uzbekistan, headquartered in Tashkent. From its hub in Tashkent it serves domestic destinations and operates international flights to Asia, Europe and North America. The airline is not a member of any airline alliance.

== History ==
The airline was formed after the dissolution of the Soviet Union. It was established on 28 January 1992 and took over the Uzbekistan division of Aeroflot on 31 May 1992.
In 1993 it received its first two Airbus A310-300 aircraft. By March 1995 the fleet consisted mostly of Soviet and Ukrainian aircraft types (An-24, An-26, Il-62, Il-76, Il-86, Tu-154, Yak-40), and it was the only airline operating in the country.
At the end of 1995 it placed its first order with Boeing, for two Boeing 767-300ER aircraft and one Boeing 757.
Uzbekistan Airways was the launch customer for the Ilyushin Il-114. The first locally assembled Il-114 was delivered in July 1998.
In 1999 the airline took ownership of the first of two directly ordered Boeing 757-200s.
In April 2000 the airline had 16,296 employees.
In 2014 it carried 2.625 million passengers, 1.9% fewer than the year before, while cargo grew by 4.6%.
After Shavkat Mirziyoyev was elected President in 2016, the airline began an expansion with the help of the World Bank. Its first Boeing 787-8 was delivered in August 2016 and was first used on flights to New York JFK.
In 2017 the airline carried 2.7 million passengers. In 2018 it carried 3.1 million passengers, of whom 1.9 million were international passengers.
In May 2018, after the Il-114 aircraft were retired, the airline moved to an all-jet fleet. The first Airbus A320neo arrived in March 2019. In 2019 new routes to Tbilisi, Munich and Karachi were added.
During the COVID-19 pandemic flights were suspended; domestic flights resumed at the end of March 2021, followed by flights to Moscow. In June 2021 Moscow flights moved from Vnukovo Airport to Domodedovo Airport.
In February 2022 the airline received its first Airbus A321LR.
According to its 2024 annual report, the airline carried more than 6 million passengers in 2024, an increase of 20%. The load factor was 83%. Profit grew by almost 70% to 687 billion Uzbek soum (about 56 million US dollars). Eleven new routes were opened in 2024, including Nha Trang and Phu Quoc in Vietnam.
At the end of 2025 the airline ordered 14 Boeing 787-9 aircraft; the order later grew to 22 after 8 options were converted. It also added four more Airbus A321neo aircraft through the lessor CALC, bringing that total to 6, with deliveries expected in 2027-2028.

== Fleet history ==
In 2007 the fleet had 55 aircraft of ten different types. By 2008 ten Airbus A320s had been ordered; the first was delivered in July 2010 and used on the Tashkent-Baku route. The first Boeing 767-300ER from the 2008 order was delivered in February 2012. The An-24 was retired in 2012 and the A310 in July 2013. Two Boeing 767-300 aircraft were converted into freighters, a project completed in 2014. The first Boeing 787-8 arrived in August 2016 with flat-bed business class seats. Two Let 410UVP aircraft were ordered in 2023, and the first was delivered in June 2023. In 2023 Airbus A330 aircraft were wet-leased from Qanot Sharq.

== Interline partners ==
Uzbekistan Airways has interline agreements with many airlines, including Aeroflot, Air Astana, airBaltic, Air France, ANA, Asiana, Azerbaijan Airlines, Belavia, China Southern, Delta, Etihad, flydubai, Iberia, Japan Airlines, KLM, Korean Air, Lufthansa, Malaysia Airlines, Qantas, Qatar Airways, S7, Singapore Airlines, Thai Airways and Turkish Airlines.

== Accidents and incidents ==
According to the Aviation Safety Network, as of January 2013 Uzbekistan Airways had experienced eight accidents with a total of 54 fatalities. Notable events:
- 17 June 1995, near Nukus: an An-2R (UK-33058) crashed 43 km from the city.
- 26 August 1999, Turtkul: a Yak-40 (UK-87848) struck power lines and made a belly landing after a second go-around; 2 of the 33 people on board were killed.
- 13 January 2004, Tashkent: a Yak-40 (UK-87985) operating Flight 1154 from Termez overran the runway and broke up; all 37 people on board were killed.
- 19 October 2006, Aranchi: an An-2TP (UK-70152) crashed in bad weather during a military training flight; all 15 people on board were killed.
- August 2009, Zarafshan: on an An-24RV (UK-46658) the landing gear was retracted too early during takeoff; there were no fatalities.
